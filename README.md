# CALL CENTER INTERVIEW — ENGLISH PREP
### Teleperformance · Interpreter campaign (work from home)

These are written the way people actually talk — with contractions and
pauses. If you read them, **read them slowly.** Fast reading sounds like
reading. Slow reading sounds like thinking.

**Three rules:**
1. Speak slower than feels natural.
2. Short sentences. If you get lost, stop and start a new one.
3. If you don't understand: *"Could you repeat that, please?"* That's normal.

**Your best card:** you already did interpreting at Samsung, unpaid and
unasked, whenever a Spanish-only customer had to be transferred. Almost no
other applicant can say that. Use it.

>

>
> I handled around twenty interactions a day, and I was above target on handle

## 5. Tell me about a difficult customer

> Oh man, every day. That's just the job, right?
>
> But okay, one that I remember. This guy calls, and he's already mad before I
> say anything, because it was his third call for the same problem. And I get
> it — I'd be mad too.
>
> So I just let him talk. I didn't interrupt, I didn't try to explain anything
> yet, because when someone is that upset they're not listening anyway. When
> he finished, I told him look, I understand, and here's exactly what I'm
> going to do.
>
> We fixed it on that call, and at the end he actually thanked me. Which
> doesn't always happen, so that one stuck with me.
>
> The thing I learned is you have to let them get it out first. People calm
> down when they feel like somebody's actually listening.

**The most important answer in the interview.** Shape: *the problem → what I
did → how it ended → what I learned.*

# INTERPRETER-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS

This campaign will probably ask things a normal call center wouldn't.

**Have you done interpreting before?**
> Not as an official position, no. But at Samsung I did it constantly —
> whenever a customer only spoke Spanish and I had to transfer them, I was the
> one translating in the middle. So the actual work is familiar to me.

**What would you do if you don't know a word?**
> I'd ask for clarification instead of guessing. In interpreting you can't
> invent something that sounds close — if I'm not sure, I ask. It's better to
> take two extra seconds than to pass along the wrong thing.

**Interpreting is not the same as explaining. How do you feel about that?**
> I understand the difference. In interpreting my job is to pass the message,
> not to add my own words or give my opinion. In support I used to explain
> things — here I'd need to stay neutral and just be the voice in between.

**Are you comfortable with sensitive or emotional calls?**
> Yeah. In support I dealt with upset people all the time. The main thing is
> staying calm and not letting my own reaction get into it.

# THE PAST TENSE TRAP

They'll ask something casual to hear if you can use past tense. It sounds
friendly. It's a test.

- What did you do yesterday?
- What did you do last weekend?
- Tell me about your last vacation.

**The trick:** the FIRST verb carries the past. After that, relax.

## Irregular verbs

| Now | Past |
|---|---|
| go | **went** |
| do | **did** |
| have | **had** |
| eat | **ate** |
| see | **saw** |
| get | **got** |
| take | **took** |
| make | **made** |
| come | **came** |
| spend | **spent** |
| sleep | **slept** |
| leave | **left** |
| buy | **bought** |
| meet | **met** |

Regular verbs take **-ed**: worked, helped, studied, watched, talked, called,
finished, played.

## Sample answers

**What did you do yesterday?**
> Yesterday I woke up early and studied for a few hours. I worked on a
> programming project, and later I went out with my family. Normal day.

**What did you do last weekend?**
> I stayed home most of the weekend. I watched some videos, I studied, and on
> Sunday I visited my family. We ate together and talked for a while.

**Tell me about your last vacation.**
> I went to Saona Island with my family. It's a small island on the east side,
> you take a boat to get there.
>
> We spent the whole day at the beach, we ate there, and we came back in the
> afternoon. The water is really clear, it's beautiful. I didn't do much,
> honestly — that was the point.

**Don't say** *"I did go"* or *"I did went."* Just **"I went."**

**After did / didn't, the verb goes back to normal:**
- ✅ I **didn't go** — ❌ I didn't went
- ✅ Did you **see** it? — ❌ Did you saw it?
